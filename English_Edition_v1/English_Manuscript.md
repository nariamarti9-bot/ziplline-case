Case 1 — Climb

The Elevator Saboteur

THE CASE

No sooner has LZ Viktoria reached its cruising altitude of three thousand meters than a sudden shudder runs through the entire ship. The bow plunges sharply for a few seconds. Crystal glasses clink in the dining room before the airship steadies itself again.

Commander von Berg immediately summons me to the lower walkway—the narrow aluminum catwalk running through the dark keel space from the bow to the tail assembly.

Ship’s Engineer Lotte Lindner shines her flashlight on the elevator’s rear cable mechanism.

Engineer Lindner: “That was no material failure. Someone loosened the locknut on the upper control cable’s turnbuckle just a few minutes ago. If I had not noticed the pressure drop on the gauge immediately, we would no longer be able to control the elevator.”

Immediately in front of the elevator’s cable mechanism, a shallow pool of bright red hydraulic oil gleams on the ridged aluminum floor. It has dripped from a valve directly above the mechanism. At the edge of the pool is the smeared impression of a boot toe pointing toward the rear service airlock.

Exactly four crew members were in the rear keel section during the twenty minutes before the incident. All four wear heavy work boots with hobnailed soles.

Case 3 — Dense Fog

The Waiter with the Steady Hand

THE CASE

During the first formal dinner in the main dining room, a powerful crosswind catches LZ Viktoria over the English Channel. For almost three minutes, the airship lists noticeably to port—toward the windows on the left.

Banker Theodor Vance sits at Table 3, his back to the narrow passage beside the windows. Beneath the tabletop, he has hung his flat morocco-leather document case on a sturdy brass spring hook. The case contains confidential construction contracts belonging to the airship company.

Leaning directly beside the brass hook is Vance’s own billiard cue. He used it in the lounge before dinner and has freshly coated its tip with blue chalk.

When the airship levels out and Vance reaches for his case, the spring hook under the table is empty.

Banker Vance: “Before the crosswind hit, the case was securely on its hook! Opening that stiff spring clip takes a free hand and firm pressure from a thumb. And whoever reached under my table must inevitably have brushed the freshly chalked tip of my billiard cue!”

Four stewards were on duty in the dining room during the brief list: Polzin, Gärtner, Lind and Marek.

Case 5 — Night Flight

A Spark in the Smoking Lounge

THE CASE

On a hydrogen-filled airship, an open flame is the greatest danger. The only place aboard where smoking is allowed is the slightly pressurized smoking lounge at the rear of the starboard deck.

The only entrance is through an electrically interlocked safety airlock with two heavy doors—Outer Door A and Inner Door B. No matches or personal lighters are permitted in the lounge. All cigars must be lit at the electric glow-coil lighter chained to the bar.

At 23:10, Bar Steward Hobi notices a burning smell while clearing the rear corner table. A heap of black paper ash smolders in the brass ashtray. Beside it, the green felt tabletop bears a fresh oval scorch mark that smells strongly of lighter fluid.

Steward Hobi: “No one has used the electric lighter at the bar in the last fifteen minutes! Someone smuggled a forbidden gasoline lighter aboard, burned a document at the corner table and briefly put the hot lighter down on the tablecloth!”

Among the flakes of ash, we recover an intact paper corner. The front reads in red ink: “Meeting in the chart room: 23:40.” On the back is written “October 16, 1934.” The meeting is therefore scheduled for the third evening of the voyage.

Case 9 — High Stakes

The Rigged Game in the Lounge

THE CASE

Every evening, high-stakes card games take place in the rear music lounge. An elegant passenger named Mr. Feddersen wins against the merchant Lasker with such uncanny certainty that Lasker asks me to watch.

The rules could hardly be simpler: each player receives a single face-down card and looks only at his own hand. They then bet. A player who folds loses his initial stake; if both stay in, they reveal their cards, and the higher card wins. The ace is highest.

The deck was freshly unsealed from its tax band in front of me. The dealer changes after each round, and the backs of the cards are completely unmarked.

Steward Bruno serves the card table. While pouring drinks, he passes behind both players and can see their cards when they briefly lift them. Only afterward does he put Feddersen’s freshly filled water glass on the table—sometimes to the left of the hand holding his card, sometimes to the right.

Case 10 — Night Flight in the Storm

The Switched Cabin

THE CASE

At 01:00 on the second stormy night, someone breaks into Cabin A5. Its occupant, a respectable engineer from the Aviation Ministry, sleeps soundly while his briefcase is searched and a technical investigation report stolen.
